#+title: BMAP on the QuietComfort Ultra Headphones (2nd Gen)

What =bosectl= knows about BMAP, Bose's control protocol, and how it learned
it. Firmware 10.12.12 unless stated otherwise.

The starting point was the work of several open-source projects that took the
protocol apart, from Bose's apps and from captures. Their findings disagree in
places and some do not hold on this model, so everything below was checked
against real headphones, and the notes say where this firmware differs from
what is commonly assumed. Where a detail is still a guess, it says so.

* Transport

BMAP runs over Bluetooth Classic RFCOMM. The headphones publish it as a plain
Serial Port service (UUID =0x1101=); the 128-bit BMAP UUID that tools on other
platforms search for is not among the SDP records macOS caches. =bosectl=
walks =IOBluetoothDevice.services= for a Serial Port record and takes its
RFCOMM channel number. (Asking IOBluetooth for a record by the 128-bit UUID is
worse than useless: it matched a Sony headset.)

Opening the channel brings the baseband link up if it is down, which wakes the
headphones and hands them the Mac's audio. That is why =bosectl= checks
=isConnected= before opening anything and never connects implicitly.

The first write on a fresh channel sometimes fails with =kIOReturnUnderrun=. A
refused write sends nothing, so retrying it is safe; =bosectl= tries four
times, 0.1 s apart and lengthening.

The headphones serve one RFCOMM control session at a time, across hosts: while
a linked phone (presumably the Bose app) held it, every attempt from the Mac
failed with =kIOReturnError= (0xe00002bc). The BMAP service over Bluetooth LE
is separate and still let the Mac in (see below). Once the headphones stopped
advertising while the phone held them; another time they kept advertising.
While one process holds the channel, another on the same Mac fails to open it
the same way, about 5 s after asking, at least at first: retried for a second
or two, it sometimes gets through and sometimes does not. The same code shows
up briefly after a session closes, which is why =bosectl= tries the RFCOMM
channel once more when LE fails after a refusal.

* Framing

Every packet is

#+begin_example
[block, function, flags, length, payload...]
flags = deviceID << 6 | port << 4 | operator
#+end_example

A function is addressed by the pair (block, function), written =31.3=. The
length byte caps a payload at 255 bytes. =deviceID= and =port= are zero
throughout.

The operators:

- 0 SET, 1 GET, 2 SETGET, 5 START: requests.
- 3 STATUS: the answer to GET and SETGET, and what the headphones send
  unprompted when something changes.
- 6 RESULT: the final answer to a START.
- 7 PROCESSING: a START was accepted and is under way.
- 4 ERROR: the payload's first byte is the reason. 1 bad length, 3 block not
  supported, 4 function not supported, 5 operator not supported (also what an
  unauthenticated request gets), 6 invalid data, 7 data unavailable, 8 runtime
  error, 10 invalid state, 15 invalid transition, 20 insecure transport.

A SETGET writes and answers with the stored value. =bosectl= compares that echo
with what it asked for and reports a write that did not stick, rather than
trusting the acknowledgement: on this firmware some writes are acknowledged and
ignored (see voice prompts).

A START answers in one of three ways, and the client has to know which:

- RESULT, possibly after PROCESSING (31.3, switching modes).
- PROCESSING only, with the effect visible later (4.1 and 4.2, host
  connections). Waiting for a RESULT here only times out.
- A series of STATUS packets for /other/ functions (31.1, which lists every
  mode as one 31.6 record each, then goes quiet).

A STATUS push for the same function can land while a START is waiting. It is
not the answer.

* Authentication

Bose's app authenticates with a challenge in block 18 before it changes
anything. Without that, most writes fail with error 5. On this model, though,
a lot is open:

- Every GET.
- SETGET on all of block 1 and block 31, on 5.5 (volume) and on 9.2
  (notifications).
- START on 31.3 (switch mode), 4.1, 4.2 (host connect and disconnect), 4.8
  (pairing mode) and 7.4 (power).

That covers everything =bosectl= does, so it does not authenticate, and it
never uses plain SET, which is the operator authentication gates.

* Functions

The payload layouts below are the ones =bosectl= reads and writes. Bytes are
numbered from 0; =?= marks bytes whose meaning is unknown.

** Block 0: product information

- 0.1 BMAP version, ASCII.
- 0.3 product id: =[id hi, id lo, variant]=. The QC Ultra 2 is =0x4082=. The
  variant is the colour: 1 Black, 2 White Smoke, 3 Driftwood Sand, 4 Midnight
  Violet, 5 Desert Gold. =Sources/BMAP/Catalog.swift= lists other product
  ids from the same sources; only =0x4082= has been seen.
- 0.5 firmware version, ASCII.
- 0.7 serial number, ASCII.
- 0.15 model name, ASCII.

** Block 1: settings

- 1.2 name. GET: =[?, name...]=. SETGET with the bare UTF-8 name, at most 30
  bytes.
- 1.3 voice prompts. GET: 7 bytes. Byte 0 carries the language in bits 0 to 4,
  connection announcements in bit 5 and "this is the default language" in bit
  6; byte 6 is the power-on battery announcement. SETGET takes
  =[bit 5 | language, battery announcement]=. *On 10.12.12 the write is
  acknowledged and ignored*: the echo carries the old value.
- 1.5 the old noise level register. Read-only here; noise control moved to
  block 31.
- 1.7 equalizer. GET: one =[min, max, current, band]= group per band, signed,
  bands 0 bass, 1 mid, 2 treble, range -10 to 10. SETGET =[value, band]= sets
  one band.
- 1.9 shortcut button. GET: =[button, event, action, supported...]=, where
  =supported= is a big-endian u32 in which bit /n/ means action /n/ is
  accepted. (Read little-endian it lists actions the app never offers and
  leaves out the configured one.) On this model the button accepts vpa,
  disabled, spotify-go and spatial-audio-mode. SETGET
  =[button, event, action]=.
- 1.10 multipoint: bit 0 of byte 0. The other bits are not ours to change, so
  =bosectl= reads, flips bit 0 and writes the byte back.
- 1.11 sidetone. GET =[flags, level]=, SETGET =[persist, level]=. Level 0 off, 1
  high, 2 medium, 3 low.
- 1.24 auto-pause and 1.27 auto-answer: one byte, 0 or 1.

** Block 2: status

- 2.2 battery: =[level, 0xff, 0xff, 0x00]=. The level is in percent, and
  0xFF means no reading. The other three bytes stay the same on the cable and
  off it. Every level seen so far has been a multiple of 10.
- 2.5 charger: =[1]= while a charging cable is connected, =[0]= otherwise.
  Pushed when it changes, and it bounces as the plug goes in (=01 00 01=
  within a second). It says the cable is there, not that the battery is
  filling, so =bosectl= reports a full battery on the cable as charged.
  Each of its pushes has arrived twice; battery pushes arrive once.

** Block 4: the hosts the headphones remember

- 4.4 remembered hosts: =[?, address, address...]=, six bytes each. The order
  changes between reads, so nothing may be keyed on a position.
- 4.5 one host: GET with the address as payload, answer
  =[address, status, ?, ?, name...]=. Bit 0 of =status= is "linked now".
- 4.9 the address of the host asking, which is how =bosectl= finds this Mac in
  the list.
- 4.1 connect a host: START =[0x00, address]=. Answers PROCESSING and nothing
  more; poll 4.5 until the host shows as linked. =bosectl hosts connect= waits
  up to 15 s by default.
- 4.2 disconnect a host: START =[address]=, answered with PROCESSING.
  Disconnecting the Mac that asks is pointless: macOS pages straight back.
- 4.8 pairing mode: GET =[state]=; START =[1]= enters it and =[0]= leaves it,
  answered with PROCESSING and then RESULT =[state, 1]=. Entering it drops the
  second of two linked hosts, presumably to free a slot; this Mac kept its
  link and the phone was the one dropped.

Commonly listed but absent here: 4.12, routing audio to a chosen host. START
=[0x82, address]= answers "function not supported". With two hosts linked,
the one that starts playing takes over.

** Block 5: audio

- 5.1 audio source: =[?, ?, n, address...]=. Byte 2 is either the source kind
  (1 Bluetooth, 2 aux) or the number of addresses; with one Bluetooth host the
  two readings agree, so =bosectl= takes every whole address that follows.
- 5.3 media control: START =[action]=, 0 stop, 1 play, 2 pause, 3 next, 4
  previous. A GET returns the supported actions as a bit mask. During
  playback a pause pauses; with nothing playing it answers "invalid data",
  though the payload is the same. There is no toggle action and no readable
  play state, so =bosectl media toggle= sends a pause and falls back to play
  on that error.
- 5.4 codec, read only.
- 5.5 volume: GET and SETGET answer =[max, level]=; SETGET takes =[level]=. The
  level is the AVRCP absolute volume, so it moves with the Mac's own volume
  slider.

** Block 7: control

- 7.4 power: START =[0x00]= turns the headphones off. Nothing turns them back
  on remotely.

** Block 9: notifications

Without a subscription the headphones push nothing. 9.2 SETGET
=[op, mask]= subscribes, where =op= is 1 enable, 2 disable or 0 overwrite and
=mask= is a big-endian u32 in which bit /n/ means block /n/. The subscription
lasts as long as the channel. =bosectl watch= subscribes to blocks 1, 2, 4, 5
and 31.

** Block 18: authentication

Its GETs are open and carry two identifiers: 18.12 a codename and 18.13 a
platform name, both ASCII. =bosectl info= shows them.

** Block 31: modes

The headphones have eleven mode slots. Slots 0 to 3 are the presets (0 quiet,
1 aware, 2 immersion, 3 cinema); 4 to 10 hold custom modes.

- 31.1 START lists every slot, as one 31.6 STATUS record each.
- 31.3 the active mode. GET answers =[index]=, and 0xFF means none is active.
  START =[index, announce]= switches, with =announce= 1 to play the mode's
  voice prompt.
- 31.4 the default mode. A GET times out on this firmware.
- 31.6 one mode's record. GET with =[index]=; SETGET with the 40-byte record
  below writes it.
- 31.8 favorites, the modes the mode button cycles through:
  =[slot count, mask...]=, where the mask is stored byte-reversed: its last byte
  holds modes 0 to 7, the byte before it 8 to 15. SETGET with the same layout.
- 31.10 the live noise settings: =[cnc, auto cnc, spatial, wind block, anc]=.
  Read it, never write it (see below).

A mode record, as GET and 31.1 return it (48 bytes):

#+begin_example
[0] index        [1-2] voice prompt   [3] editable    [4] ?
[5] favorite     [6-37] name, 32 bytes, zero-terminated
[38-40] ?        [41] mutability      [42] noise level
[43] auto cnc    [44] spatial         [45] copy of [3]
[46] wind block  [47] anc
#+end_example

and as SETGET takes it, and echoes it back (40 bytes):

#+begin_example
[0] index        [1-2] voice prompt   [3-34] name, 32 bytes
[35] noise level [36] auto cnc        [37] spatial
[38] wind block  [39] anc
#+end_example

The fields:

- Noise level: 0 cancels the most, 10 lets the most in.
- Spatial: 0 off, 1 room (a fixed stage), 2 head (follows head movement).
- Mutability, byte 41: what a write may change. Bit 0 is the noise level, bit 2
  spatial audio.
- Editable, byte 3: 0 for the presets, 1 for custom modes.
- A deleted slot reads as a mode named "None" with the noise level at 10 and
  noise cancellation on. Deleting means writing that record back, keeping the
  slot's own voice prompt.

** Changing the noise settings

*Never write 31.10.* It looks like the obvious register for "change the noise
level now", and other tools write it. Writing it with a preset active detaches
the preset (31.3 then reads 0xFF) and leaves noise cancellation off.

The presets cannot change at all: a SETGET on their record fails with error 8
(runtime error). Custom modes can, so =bosectl= changes the noise level, noise
cancellation, wind block and spatial audio by a read-modify-write of the active
mode's 31.6 record, after checking the mutability bits.

Three firmware rules surfaced along the way:

- The noise level only matters while noise cancellation is on, so a level
  change has to turn it on with it, or it seems to do nothing.
- Wind block bypasses noise cancellation entirely, which makes a level change
  inaudible while it is on.
- Automatic noise level (ActiveSense) is a feature of the aware preset. A
  custom mode with it set is refused.

* Bluetooth LE advertisement

While on, the headphones advertise over Bluetooth LE, whether or not anything
is linked to them. The manufacturer data, under Bose's company identifier
=0x009E= (little-endian), reads

#+begin_example
[0x9e, 0x00, format, product, flags, six more bytes]
#+end_example

- =format= is 0, the only one understood.
- =product= holds a product value in its low 5 bits, offset by 100: the QC
  Ultra 2 sends =0x8c=, so 12 + 100 = 112, the value Bose's own product table
  gives this model. The high 3 bits are the colour, numbered as in 0.3.
- =flags= read =0x07= on the cable and =0x0f= in pairing mode: bit 0 is
  charging and bit 3 pairing mode. Bit 1 is described elsewhere as "a
  connection slot is free", but it stayed set with two hosts linked. Bit 2 is
  set and not understood.
- The last six bytes did not change across these states and are not decoded.

macOS hides LE addresses from applications, so a heard device cannot be
matched to the paired one by address; CoreBluetooth gives a per-Mac
identifier instead. The advertisement carries the headphones' name.

* BMAP over Bluetooth LE

The same packets also travel over a GATT service, =FEBE=. It has two characteristics, both
readable, writable with and without response, and notifying:

- =C65B8F2F-AEE2-4C89-B758-BC4892D6F2D8=, which needs an encrypted link. It
  worked on a Mac paired over Classic with no pairing prompt, presumably
  because the Classic bond carries over to LE. Requests written here are answered by notifications on it.
- =D417C028-9818-4354-99D1-2AC09D074591=. Writing to it failed with an
  unknown ATT error.

Every write and every notification carries one segment of a packet, behind a
one-byte segment header: the high nibble is the index of the last segment and
the low nibble this segment's index, so a packet that fits takes header =0x00=.
Each segment holds up to 19 packet bytes. The battery request and its answer
look like this on the wire:

#+begin_example
-> 00 02 02 01 00
<- 00 02 02 03 04 32 ff ff 00     (header 00, then 2.2 STATUS: 50%)
#+end_example

Using LE needs no Classic link and never moves the Mac's audio. An LE
connection from the Mac sometimes fails to come up right after a Classic
session; trying again a moment later worked.

While a phone held the RFCOMM channel, and while another =bosectl= process
held it, LE still connected, and reads and checked writes worked. =bosectl=
falls back to LE when the RFCOMM channel is refused, and =--le= uses it from
the start. Both can be open at once: settings changed over RFCOMM were pushed to a
subscriber on LE.

* What is not known yet

- The unknown bytes in the mode record, 5.1 and 4.5.
- How much of this holds on other BMAP headphones. The function numbers are
  shared across Bose's recent models; payload details may not be.
